Listening

Audio recordings with timed questions and realistic practice conditions.

4 sections 40 questions About 30 minutes Each recording is played once
What the four sections contain
  • Section 1: a conversation between two people in an everyday social situation, such as booking a service.
  • Section 2: a monologue in an everyday social context, such as a talk about local facilities.
  • Section 3: a conversation between up to four people in an education or training setting.
  • Section 4: a monologue on an academic subject, such as a university lecture.
  • The sections get harder as the recording goes on.
Question types
  • Multiple choice.
  • Matching.
  • Plan, map and diagram labelling.
  • Form, note, table, flow-chart and summary completion.
  • Sentence completion and short-answer questions.
Tips for a higher score
  • Use the preparation time to read the questions and predict the type of answer you need.
  • Check the word limit for each task. Going over it makes the answer wrong.
  • Answers follow the order of the recording. If you lose your place, move on to the next question.
  • Listen for corrections. Speakers often change their mind ("Actually, make that Thursday").
  • Spelling and singular/plural forms count, so check them.

Reading

Passages with a range of IELTS question types and timed practice.

3 sections 40 questions 60 minutes No extra time to copy answers
Academic and General Training
  • Academic: three long passages taken from books, journals, magazines and newspapers.
  • General Training: short everyday texts first (notices, adverts), then workplace texts, then one longer passage.
  • Both versions have 40 questions in 60 minutes, so manage your time carefully.
Question types
  • True / False / Not Given and Yes / No / Not Given.
  • Matching headings, information and features.
  • Sentence, summary, note, table and flow-chart completion.
  • Multiple choice and short-answer questions.
  • Diagram labelling.
Tips for a higher score
  • Spend about 20 minutes on each passage. Do not let one hard passage use up the whole hour.
  • Skim each passage first to see how it is organised, then read for detail when a question needs it.
  • "False" means the text says the opposite. "Not Given" means the text does not say either way.
  • Questions use different words from the text, so look for paraphrases and not exact matches.
  • Write your answers on the answer sheet as you go. There is no extra time at the end.

Writing

Essays and reports completed against the clock and submitted for review.

2 tasks 60 minutes Task 1: at least 150 words Task 2: at least 250 words
The two tasks
  • Academic Task 1: describe a graph, chart, table, process or map in your own words.
  • General Training Task 1: write a letter, formal, semi-formal or informal depending on the situation.
  • Task 2 (both tests): write an essay responding to a point of view, argument or problem.
  • Task 2 is worth more than Task 1, so spend about 40 minutes on it and about 20 on Task 1.
How Writing is marked
  • Task Achievement (Task 1) or Task Response (Task 2): did you answer every part of the question?
  • Coherence and Cohesion: is the writing organised into clear paragraphs and linked logically?
  • Lexical Resource: range and accuracy of vocabulary.
  • Grammatical Range and Accuracy: variety and correctness of sentence structures.
Tips for a higher score
  • Spend a few minutes planning before you write.
  • Read the question again at the end to check that you answered every part.
  • For Academic Task 1, include a clear overview of the main trends or features.
  • Give reasons and examples in Task 2 to support your opinions.
  • Do not memorise whole answers. Examiners can spot them, and they may not fit the question.
  • Leave a minute or two to check spelling, grammar and word count.

Speaking

Record your answers to examiner-style prompts and practise speaking under realistic conditions.

3 parts 11–14 minutes One-to-one with an examiner Recorded
The three parts
  • Part 1: introduction and interview, about 4–5 minutes. Questions on familiar topics such as home, work, studies and hobbies.
  • Part 2: the long turn. You get a task card and one minute to prepare, then speak for 1–2 minutes.
  • Part 3: a two-way discussion, about 4–5 minutes. Questions are more abstract and linked to the Part 2 topic.
How Speaking is marked
  • Fluency and Coherence: speaking at a natural pace and organising your ideas.
  • Lexical Resource: range of vocabulary and how precisely you use it.
  • Grammatical Range and Accuracy: variety and correctness of structures.
  • Pronunciation: clear sounds, stress and intonation.
Tips for a higher score
  • Extend your answers with a reason or an example. One-word answers give the examiner nothing to assess.
  • Use your preparation minute in Part 2 to jot down key words, not full sentences.
  • If you do not understand a question, it is fine to ask the examiner to repeat it.
  • Do not memorise scripts. Speak naturally, and correct yourself if you make a mistake.
  • Practise out loud and listen back to your recordings.

Study Tips

  1. Practise regularly, even if it's just for 30 minutes.
  2. Focus on your weaknesses and track your progress.
  3. Use authentic materials (news, podcasts, etc.).
  4. Keep a vocabulary notebook.
  5. Stay positive and be consistent!
