About IELTS

IELTS is an international English-language test that assesses four skills: Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. Your performance is reported using a nine-band scale, from Band 0 to Band 9.

IELTS is used for purposes including study, professional registration, work, migration and UK visa and immigration applications. The test you need depends on your destination, institution and purpose.

Which IELTS test do you need?

IELTS has different test types for different purposes. Always check the requirements of your university, employer, professional body or immigration authority before booking your test.

IELTS Academic Mainly designed for people planning to study at university or another higher-education institution in an English-speaking environment. It is also used for some professional registration purposes.
IELTS General Training Designed around everyday and workplace English. It is commonly used for migration, work experience, training and study below degree level.
IELTS for UKVI A secure IELTS test used for certain UK Visas and Immigration applications. UKVI requirements depend on the visa route and the level of English required.
IELTS Life Skills A UKVI test that assesses only Listening and Speaking. Unlike standard IELTS, Life Skills is reported as Pass or Fail rather than a nine-band score.

Where can IELTS take you?

IELTS results are used by organisations around the world for education, professional registration, employment and immigration.

Common destinations for IELTS candidates include:

Australia Used for study, migration and professional purposes, depending on the organisation or visa route.
Canada IELTS is used for education, immigration and other purposes where English-language evidence is required.
New Zealand Used for study, work and immigration pathways depending on the relevant requirements.
United Kingdom Used for study, work and immigration. Some visa routes require IELTS for UKVI.
United States IELTS is accepted by many universities and other organisations for demonstrating English proficiency.
Worldwide IELTS is accepted by thousands of organisations globally. Requirements vary, so check the institution or authority you are applying to.

How IELTS is scored

IELTS uses a nine-band scale. You receive an individual score for Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking, as well as an Overall Band Score calculated from the four component scores.

9 Expert user — Fully operational command of the language: appropriate, accurate and fluent with complete understanding.
8 Very good user — Fully operational command with only occasional unsystematic inaccuracies or inappropriacies.
7 Good user — Operational command of English, though occasional inaccuracies, inappropriacies and misunderstandings may occur.
6 Competent user — Generally effective command despite some inaccuracies, inappropriacies and misunderstandings.
5 Modest user — Partial command of English, coping with overall meaning in most situations.
4 Limited user — Basic competence limited mainly to familiar situations.
3 Extremely limited user — Conveys and understands only general meaning in very familiar situations.
2 Intermittent user — Great difficulty understanding spoken and written English.
1 Non-user — Essentially no ability to use English beyond isolated words.
0 Did not attempt the test — No answers were provided.

How is the Overall Band Score calculated?

Your Overall Band Score is based on your four individual section scores. IELTS calculates the average of the four scores and applies its rounding rules.

Listening: 6.5

Reading: 6.5

Writing: 5.0

Speaking: 7.0

Average: 6.25

Overall Band: 6.5

Test at a glance

Academic and General Training IELTS both assess Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. Listening and Speaking are the same in both tests, while Reading and Writing differ.

Listening Approximately 30 minutes. You listen to recordings and answer questions based on what you hear.
Reading 60 minutes. Academic and General Training use different Reading tests.
Writing 60 minutes. Academic and General Training use different Writing tasks.
Speaking Approximately 11–14 minutes. The test is conducted as a speaking interview with an examiner.

Test delivery and exact procedures can vary by test format and location. Check the official IELTS information for the test you intend to take.
